Return: Plan A (Costa Brava) vs Plan B (Ferragosto Bypass)

Connections: D49, D50, D51, D52

🎯 Plan A — Costa Brava (base itinerary)

D Date Route Km Hours Highlight
D49 Thu 13/08 Palencia ➔ Cadaqués 720 8h 30m Cap de Creus, Dalí's village
D50 Fri 14/08 Costa Brava (relax) 30 — Beaches + fishing
D51 Sat 15/08 Costa Brava ➔ Côte d'Azur 560 6h ⚠️ FERRAGOSTO = BLACK ALERT
D52 Sun 16/08 Côte d'Azur ➔ Genoa 200 4h Genoa Aquarium

Pros: Cadaqués and Cap de Creus are spectacular (lunar landscape, picturesque village).

Cons: D51 = Ferragosto on AP-7/A9 = risk of 10-12h queues. Van parking in Cadaqués almost impossible in high season. Leave at 5:00 am to mitigate.

🛡️ Plan B — Ferragosto Bypass via Zaragoza + Camargue (RECOMMENDED)

You sacrifice the Costa Brava but completely avoid Ferragosto traffic. The Camargue is an excellent alternative with children (horses, flamingos, free beaches).

D Date Route Km Hours Strategic notes
D49 Thu 13/08 Palencia ➔ Zaragoza 350 4h Calm trip post-eclipse. Afternoon tapas + Basilica del Pilar
D50 Fri 14/08 Zaragoza ➔ Camargue 🇫🇷 580 6h 30m Cross the border BEFORE the weekend. Arrival Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer evening
D51 Sat 15/08 Camargue (stationary) 0 — 🛑 Ferragosto: ZERO highway! Flamingos, white horses, free beaches
D52 Sun 16/08 Camargue ➔ Genoa 450 5h 30m Departure at 7:00 am. A8/A10 towards Genoa. Aquarium in the afternoon

Pros: Avoid black alert. Better distributed kilometers. Camargue = pure nature, perfect for children. Zaragoza is worth it (Pilar, tapas, river Aquarium).

Cons: You lose Cadaqués/Cap de Creus. D50 (580 km) is still demanding — but on Friday morning the A9 is still flowing.

📍 Camargue — what to do with children:

➕ Horse ride on the beach (many stables accept children 6+)

➕ Parc Ornithologique de Pont de Gau — flamingos 2 meters away! (~€8/adult, €5/child) 📍 Maps

➕ Plage de Beauduc or Plage de Piémanson — wild beaches (check van access 2026) 📍 Maps

➕ Fishing: Camargue canals — eel, mullet, sea bream. FREE from shore (France)

🚐 Van parking: Chemin Bas des Launes (Saintes-Maries) — tolerated area for motorhomes, ~€8-12/night in August. Arrive before 16:00! Alternative: Camping de la Brise (€25-35/night, with services).

Maps: 📍 Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer, 📍 Parc Ornithologique Pont de Gau

📝 Decision: when to choose?

Check traffic forecasts on Bison Futé 1 week before:

If 15/08 is black alert on A9/AP-7 → Plan B (Zaragoza-Camargue)

If 15/08 is only red alert and you leave at 5:00 am → Plan A feasible

Other return alternatives

➕ Carcassonne 🇫🇷: medieval citadel (+1h detour from Perpignan, combinable with Plan B) 📍 Maps

➕ Portofino 🇮🇹: only if it's not August... but it is August. 📍 Maps

🎒 Equipment

Vehicle and gas:

  • Map LiFePo4 charging points and gas cylinder exchange
  • Gas cylinder adapter EuroCombi (different fittings in Scandinavia — Primus/CGA 600)
  • 10 kg gas cylinder (or refilling plan: YX/Circle K in Norway)

Insect protection (Lapland D9–D16):

  • Magnetic mosquito nets for van
  • DEET 30%+ spray/lotion
  • Head nets (mosquito nets for head) — essential for kids!

Fishing:

  • 2.4m telescopic rod + reel
  • 3-4m fixed rod (mato-onki for kids)
  • Spinners, bobbers, hooks, line
  • Hook remover pliers + scissors

Trekking:

  • Waterproof hiking boots for all (NO sneakers!)
  • Telescopic poles (adults + child 8)
  • Lightweight rain jackets for everyone
  • Fleece for everyone (at altitude 5-10°C even in July)

Scooters:

  • 4 foldable muscle scooters (200mm wheels)
  • Helmets for children (not mandatory but recommended)
  • Lightweight lock to leave them outside shops

Solar eclipse (D48, 12/08):

  • ISO 12312-2 solar filters for eclipse (4 pairs)
  • Identify 2-3 spots in Palencia (free West horizon)

Miscellaneous:

  • Waterproof SPF50 sunscreen
  • Water shoes (kayak, Nordic beaches)
  • Waterproof bags for phones
  • 20,000 mAh powerbank
  • Emergency thermal blanket
  • Whistle for children (3 whistles = SOS)

🇳🇴 Norway — AutoPASS / SkyttelPASS (D16–D35)

The Norwegian toll system covers all toll booths, bridges, tunnels, and most internal ferries. For foreign tourists with their own vehicle:

Option Discount Cost How
No registration 0% (invoice at home +20%) — License plate read automatically, invoice sent
SkyttelPASS tag 20% tolls + 10% ferries NOK 200 (~€19) one-time Order 4–6 weeks in advance, shipped home
+ AutoPASS ferje prepayment 50% ferries Deposit NOK 3,600 (~€333) refundable Add after receiving the tag

Estimated trip savings (19 days in Norway): tolls ~NOK 2,500 → 20% discount = ~€43 saved; internal ferries ~NOK 2,000 → 50% discount = ~€87 saved. Total: ~€130 saved (net of tag cost).

⚠️ Warning: the tag must be ordered in advance (postal shipment). The agreement activates 30 minutes after registration (license plate read even without physical tag). The ferje deposit is refundable upon agreement closure.

⚠️ Logistical Risks — Critical Points

1. "The Baltic Marathon" (D3–D6)

This is the real test of the trip. On paper it seems doable, but in reality it is a stress test for the children:

🚗 D3: Vienna → Warsaw (670 km, 8h 30m)

🚗 D4: Warsaw → Vilnius (510 km, 6h)

🚗 D5: Vilnius → Riga (300 km, 4h)

🚗 D6: Riga ➔ Pärnu ➔ Haapsalu ➔ Tallinn (390 km, 5h)

The problem: You will drive about 1,870 km across 4 driving days (D3–D6), with D7 as a rest day in Tallinn. Considering bathroom breaks, lunches, and traffic, actual travel times will increase by 20-30%. The children will spend most of the time strapped in car seats.

Solution: Always start at dawn (e.g. 5:00 or 6:00 AM). The children will sleep for the first 3-4 hours of travel, allowing you to "cover kilometers" peacefully and arrive at the destination by lunchtime, saving afternoons for parks and explorations.

2. Leaving the Lofoten (D25: Lofoten → Mo i Rana)

The table shows 400 km, 5h + ferry. Be careful as this estimate is optimistic:

The Moskenes–Bodø ferry lasts about 3.5–4 hours

You must be at the boarding at least 1 hour (or 2) before

From Bodø to Mo i Rana there are about 230 km (another 3.5 hours driving)

The problem: This will be a draining day, almost exclusively transit (about 9-10 hours total including driving, waiting, and sailing). Consider it a "lost" day tourist-wise and prepare onboard entertainment for the children.

3. The Ferry Uncertainty (Mandatory Reservations)

The trip relies on some crucial ferries (Tallinn–Helsinki, Moskenes–Bodø, Kristiansand–Hirtshals).

In August and late July, the Moskenes–Bodø route is overwhelmed by motorhome travelers from all over Europe. If you show up at boarding without a reservation, you might have to wait 24 or 48 hours in line (in the "standby lane").

⚠️ You absolutely must book these ferries 2-3 months in advance, which will bind you to strictly follow the schedule up to D25.

The Kristiansand–Hirtshals ferry (D36) is less critical but booking is still recommended in high season.

4. Ferragosto in Genoa (D51–D53)

As noted in the file, the A10 highway at Ferragosto is hell. Being in Genoa on 17/08 (D53) saves you from the absolute peak, but expect very heavy slowdowns in Liguria.

Tip: On 16/08 (D52) from Camargue to Genoa (450 km, A8/A10) — leave at 7:00 AM to avoid the bulk of return traffic. Consider stopping in Savona/Finale Ligure if traffic is blocked.
